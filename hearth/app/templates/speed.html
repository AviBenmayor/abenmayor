Does responding faster predict a higher win rate?

Short answer: less than the raw cut says, and not in a way I would buy a speed-to-lead SLA on. The cliff is real, but it is at three days, and it is mostly persistence wearing a stopwatch.

2. Control A — reps call the good leads first

Response time is not assigned at random. Leads answered inside five minutes average a legacy score of 47.8 and are 57% Low Value; leads left more than three days average 33.6 and are 83% Low Value. Reps triage. So the raw gradient is partly just which leads got picked up quickly.

Reweighting each bucket to the overall legacy-score decile distribution — comparing like with like — collapses most of it. The <5m bucket goes from {{ '%.1f'|format(s.buckets[0].win) }}% to {{ '%.1f'|format(s.buckets[0].adj) }}%, and the ordering between everything from five minutes to three days stops being monotone at all.

3. Control B — an off-hours lead cannot be answered fast

Only 28% of leads arrive inside business hours. Median response is 44 minutes in-hours against 733 minutes off-hours. “Slow response” partly encodes “arrived at 3am”, which is a property of the lead, not of the rep. Split the analysis by arrival window and the in-hours and off-hours gradients look the same shape, which is what you would expect if arrival timing were doing part of the work.

4. The decomposition that actually matters

Hold effort roughly fixed — bucket leads into terciles of how many touches they eventually got — and response time stops explaining much. Effort explains nearly everything.

Read down a column, not across a row: within any effort level the response-time gradient is small and non-monotone. Read across a row and the effect is enormous. The >3d bucket averages 13.1 touches against ~27 everywhere else — those leads were not answered slowly, they were never really worked.

Would I act on it? Not as a speed SLA on its own. The honest causal read is that touch counts are endogenous — a lead that picks up the phone gets called more, not the reverse — so even the effort column is an upper bound, and nothing here is a randomised comparison.

What I would act on is the tail. The >3d bucket is 481 leads winning 7.1%, and they are distinguishable at intake: 83% Low Value, bottom-quintile legacy score. That is not a lead that needs to be called faster, it is a lead that should have been tiered down on day one so the hours went somewhere else. Routing fixes it; a stopwatch does not.

And if you want the speed question answered properly: randomise the dialer order within a score band for two weeks. That is the only version of this that supports a causal claim, and it costs nothing to run.
