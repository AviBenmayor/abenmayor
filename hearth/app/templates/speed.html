Does responding faster predict a higher win rate?

Yes — but about a third of it is reps calling the good leads first, and the real cliff is at three days, where leads weren't answered slowly so much as never worked.

"Quality-adjusted" reweights each bucket to the same legacy-score mix, so it compares like with like. Leads answered in five minutes average a legacy score of 47.8; leads left three days average 33.6. Reps triage, and the raw gradient is partly that. The {{ '{:,}'.format(s.n_untouched) }} leads never touched are excluded — they win 0% by construction.

Would I act on it? Enforce a same-business-day first touch — cheap, and the evidence supports it. Don't buy a speed-to-lead product on the raw {{ '%.1f'|format(s.headline.fast_win) }}% vs {{ '%.1f'|format(s.headline.slow_win) }}%: a lead that lands at 3am waits 17× longer for reasons unrelated to its quality and wins only 3 points less.

The >3-day bucket is the real finding. Those leads got 13 touches against ~27 everywhere else, and they're identifiable at intake — 83% Low Value, bottom-quintile legacy score. They didn't need to be called faster; they needed to be tiered down on day one. Routing fixes that. A stopwatch doesn't.
