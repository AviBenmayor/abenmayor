Reading this honestly

The “expected wins” column holds each lead's P(win) fixed. It answers if we only work the top X%, what share of the wins that happen today do we still get to? It does not claim that touching a lead 30 times instead of {{ cap.touch_cost }} raises its probability. Effort in the training data is endogenous — engaged leads get called more, not the reverse — so any model of effort→outcome built on it would overstate the return, and I would rather say that than sell you a lift number I cannot defend.

Which is why the intensity ladder is the part I would test rather than assume, and why the rollout below has a control group in it.

Where I would put the line, and why there

The constraint, measured rather than assumed. {{ cap.distinct_reps }} reps logged activity in the training window. 98% of it falls Monday to Friday, so the medians are weekday-only: {{ cap.core_reps }} reps working on a typical weekday at a median {{ cap.touches_per_rep_day }} touches each — {{ '{:,}'.format(cap.core_reps * cap.touches_per_rep_day) }} touches a day across the floor. A worked lead absorbs a median {{ cap.touch_cost }} touches, so the floor can properly work {{ '%.0f'|format(cap.leads_per_day) }} new leads per business day against {{ '%.0f'|format(cap.arriving_per_bday) }} arriving. That is {{ '%.0f'|format(cap.workable_share * 100) }}% of what marketing buys.

Both sides are counted in the same unit, which is the part that is easy to get wrong. Measuring supply in dials (50,445 of them) while pricing a lead in touches — calls, email and SMS together — understates capacity by about a third; quoting supply per working day against arrivals per calendar day then overstates coverage by about a third in the other direction. The two errors nearly cancel, which is how a wrong derivation survives a sanity check.

So why does the line sit at 30% and not {{ '%.0f'|format(cap.workable_share * 100) }}%? Because working more leads and working them properly are competing uses of the same touches. Today a lead that wins 46% and a lead that wins 2% both get the flat ~{{ cap.touch_cost }}. The ladder above spends {{ '{:,}'.format(used) }} touches against a supply of {{ '{:,}'.format(supply) }} — {{ '%.0f'|format(used / supply * 100) }}% utilisation — by giving tier A a 30-touch cadence instead of {{ cap.touch_cost }}. Working {{ '%.0f'|format(cap.workable_share * 100) }}% flat and working 30% with a real ladder cost the floor the same day; the second is worth more.

The D line sits at the bottom {{ '%.0f'|format(dropped.share) }}%: {{ '{:,}'.format(dropped.n) }} leads carrying {{ '%.1f'|format(dropped.pct_wins) }}% of expected wins, at a mean P(win) of {{ '%.1f'|format(dropped.mean_p) }}%. Giving up that {{ '%.1f'|format(dropped.pct_wins) }}% is what funds the ladder. If you tell me a touch costs more or less than I have measured, move the boxes above and the line moves with it — that is the point of the form. The model does not move with it.

What happens below the line: tier D is not deleted. It goes to marketing automation and is re-scored the moment it does anything — opens, replies, revisits pricing — at which point it re-enters the queue at its new tier. A lead that answers an email is no longer a bottom-tier lead.

How you would know in 30 days

Hold back a random 10% of tier D and work it exactly as today. That control is the only way to learn what the cutoff actually costs, and it turns the assumption above into a measurement inside one cycle. At 30 days, read four things:

  • Suppression cost — win rate in the held-out D control vs suppressed D. If the control materially outperforms, the line is too high.
  • Intensity return — win rate in tier A vs the same score decile in the Mar–May baseline. This is the only number that tests whether a 30-touch cadence beats the flat {{ cap.touch_cost }}.
  • Efficiency — touches per won deal, overall. This should fall even if total wins are flat; that is the whole point.
  • Override rate — how often a rep works a C or D anyway, and whether they were right. A high override rate that wins is the model being wrong, and I want to find that out from the reps rather than argue with them.
