Model card

What the score is, how well it does out of time, and what in the data I'd want you to know about before trusting it.

The target is expected revenue: P(win) × E[amount | win]. That's the number in submission.csv, and the tier thresholds are dollar amounts frozen into the model. The three candidate targets barely reorder this book (ρ = 0.997 against P(win), 0.974 against P(win ≤30d)), so I chose on principle: this is a revenue business, and a rep's next call should go to the lead most likely to bring in the most money.

Three fields: {% for f in m.features.categorical %}{{ f }}, {% endfor %}{{ m.features.numeric[0] }}. Fifteen parameters; any lead can be scored by hand. I dropped campaign, time zone and zip3 — they'd have bought AUC, but each either failed a stability test out of period or couldn't be tested, and they're the fields that drift most into the scoring window. At the 30% cutoff they'd have caught the same number of wins.

Trained on the 4,574 leads a rep actually worked, not all 5,507. A rep asks "if I call this, will it convert?", not "what share converted including the ones nobody phoned." That moved the probability level by ~3 points and the ranking not at all (Spearman {{ '%.3f'|format(m.selection_rho) }} against the unfiltered fit).

Out of time, never a random split — the base rate climbs 12.3% → 20.1% across the training cohorts, so a random split trains on the future. The baseline is legacy_score, the CRM score nobody uses; it carries real signal, and it's my guess at the naive baseline you're measuring against. I shipped the logistic model over gradient boosting because the gap is inside noise on 907 events and the additive model extrapolates more safely onto a shifted mix.
