## 2. The demographic read for an operator

**The headline is that the two blocks have the same number of households and completely different
households.** `homes_400m` is 4,003 at Graham and 3,982 at Roebling — a 0.5% difference. Everything
that separates the two sites is *who* those households are and *who walks past*.

**Inside a 5-minute walk (≈400 m, the pinned reach distance).** 4,003 households, median income
**$142,054 ± $12,956**. That MOE is 9.1% of the estimate — **materially tighter than Roebling's
13.4%**, and it is the single most reliable demographic number in this memo. The block is q4, not
q5, and it is q4 robustly: one standard error in either direction ($134,178–$149,930) stays inside
the q4 band (94,511–155,925), so the revenue model's spend assignment does not flip. It is a
**73%-of-Roebling income block with 21 more households**.

The household *shape* is the operator-relevant difference:

- **18–34s are 40.3% ± 6.3 here vs 57.7% ± 11.6 at Roebling.** In absolute terms this is still a
  young block — 40% is well above the Brooklyn norm — but it is not the Bedford Ave monoculture.
- **Under-18s are 13.8% vs 8.6%, over-65s 6.4% vs 1.8%, average household size 1.98 vs 1.73, and
  one-person households 34.4% vs 48.9%.** This is the whole read in four numbers: **Graham has
  families and older residents; Roebling has singles.** A 1.98-person household buys dinner for two
  or a family; a 1.73-person household buys a single ticket.
- Renters 74.7% vs 87.1%; college 69.9% vs 88.0%; Hispanic 20.8% vs 13.8%. Graham retains more of
  the pre-gentrification East Williamsburg population.

**Inside a 10-minute walk (≈800 m).** 14,999 households vs Roebling's 17,976, and the *ratio*
matters more than the level: Graham's 800 m ring holds **3.75×** its 400 m ring, Roebling's holds
**4.51×**. Graham's catchment thins out faster — it is closer to the Newtown Creek industrial edge
and the BQE. `res_area_400m` is **91.4% of all built floor area** at Graham against 81.4% at
Roebling, and `jobs_400m` is 1,789 against 3,973. **Graham is a residential block with a retail
spine through it; Roebling is a retail district with residents in it.** D82 scores both
`retail_mixed`, but Graham's `retail_area_400m` is 6.4% of built area against Roebling's 14.3%.

**What the transit profile says about dayparts — this is the most decision-relevant finding.**
`transit_am_pm_share_400m` is **1.413** at Graham and **1.056** at Roebling. Per D76 that is a
station-type signal, and the two stations are different types:

- Graham's weekday entries peak in the **AM (2,690, 36.3% of the day)** and collapse in the evening
  (**1,039, 14.0%**). Its **weekend index is 0.75** — Saturday and Sunday are *quieter* than a
  weekday.
- Roebling's weekday evening entries are **6,770 (24.2%)**, and its **weekend index is 1.06** —
  Saturday (32,491) is *busier* than a weekday.

**Graham Ave is a commuter-origin station: residents leave in the morning and come home in the
evening. Bedford Ave is a destination station: outsiders arrive, and they arrive most on Saturday.**
In absolute terms Graham sees **1,039 weekday evening entries against Bedford's 6,770 — one-sixth**
— and 2.27 M annual riders against 8.76 M, 0.56× even of Metropolitan/Lorimer's 4.08 M.

**Implications, stated plainly.**

1. **Ticket size should be *lower* per head than Roebling but *more items per ticket*.** Income is
   0.67× and the CEX-implied restaurant spend per household is 0.61×, but household size is 1.14×
   and under-18 share is 1.60×. A family-sized order at a moderate price beats a high single ticket
   here. The published menu already matches this: $5–6 empanadas with $15–19 sandwiches and $18–32
   platters is a basket-building menu, not a single-ticket menu.
2. **The daypart that exists here is the resident evening, not the visitor evening, and it is
   thin.** 1,039 weekday evening subway entries is not a nightlife corridor. But those entries are
   *residents coming home to 4,003 households* — a takeaway-dinner and family-meal daypart, not a
   drinking one. The 11:00–22:00 posted hours already capture it.
3. **Weekends are the weak spot, not the strong one.** The weekend index of 0.75 is the inverse of
   Roebling's 1.06. Any plan that assumes a weekend brunch rush is assuming Bedford Ave's traffic
   pattern at a station that does not have it.
4. **Alcohol: the D63 bar age-fit supports a licence on the ranking, but weakly, and the corridor
   signal is the binding constraint.** `age_fit_bar` is **1.118 ± 0.070** at Graham — above 1.0 by
   1.7 standard errors on the D63 SLA-composition curve, so the 40.3% 18–34 share does raise the
   bar-demand ranking here, just less than Roebling's 1.227 ± 0.133. The corridor evidence is more
   equivocal: **92 active on-premises licences within 400 m (23.0 per 1,000 households) against
   Roebling's 211 (53.0)** — and the sharpest split is **"Additional Bar": 3 at Graham vs 36 at
   Roebling**, because that class marks large multi-bar venues. Graham has restaurants that serve
   drinks; Roebling has drinking venues. **Read: a beer-and-wine licence to lift the dinner ticket
   is supported. A bar programme is not.** D63 is explicit that `age_fit` informs *ranking* and is
   non-filtering — it is not a licence recommendation.

## 4. The shipped model's read of this address

`revenue-v0` restaurant: β = 0.5, γ = **−0.25 (agglomerative)**, gate **pass** (LOZO backtest over
73 ZIPs, Spearman_oos 0.7631 vs baselines 0.5734 county-average and 0.6294 homes-only, R²_oos
0.5769). Restaurant is the **only** one of fifteen categories that ships a revenue number.

The decomposition of the Graham-vs-Roebling gap is worth stating because it is not what an operator
would guess:

| | Graham | Roebling | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| demand pool (homes × CEX spend by quintile) | $16,671,855 | $27,104,638 | **0.615** |
| implied capture × λ (p50 ÷ pool) | 7.69% | 9.21% | **0.836** |
| **p50 revenue** | **$1,282,676** | $2,494,887 | **0.514** |

**About four-fifths of the shortfall is the income quintile** (q4 $4,164.84/household vs q5
$6,806.79 on near-identical household counts). **The remaining fifth is the model rewarding
Roebling for having more competitors** — γ = −0.25 is agglomerative, so 104 restaurants within 400 m
earn a higher capture share than 44. That is the same mechanism the Lion's Milk memo flagged as
suspect for café (where γ = −0.75 produced a capture share of 6.72×). Here it is four times weaker
and the category passed its gate and its placebo, so I do not discard it — but an operator should
know that **the model says this site is worth less partly because it has fewer competitors**, and
should not treat that fifth as a site-quality statement.

**The known failure mode carries over: `revenue-v0` has no capacity term.** No seats, no square
feet, no hours, no ticket. At p50 the model implies **$933/sq ft** on PLUTO's whole-lot 1,375 sq ft
and **$1,603/sq ft** on a realistic 800 sq ft demise. Independent NYC restaurants run $210–$365/sq
ft; a high-volume small-footprint counter might reach $700–$900. **Both figures are above that band,
the 800 sq ft figure by roughly 2×.** The diagnosis from the Lion's Milk file — mean-anchored λ
applied to a median-ish shop, with revenue linear in the pool and no ceiling — applies here
unchanged, and it is why my own estimate in §6 is below the model.

## 5. "How could he make more money" — levers ranked by the size of the number behind them

Ranked by the dollar or count magnitude of the evidence, not by my confidence. Each is marked
**evidence-backed** (a number in this memo supports it) or **judgment** (my inference).

### Lever 1 — Own the resident evening and the family dinner. **Evidence-backed.**
**The number: 1,039 weekday evening subway entries returning to 4,003 households of average size
1.98 with a 13.8% under-18 share, against a weekend index of 0.75.**

This is the block's structural asset and it is the opposite of Roebling's. Graham's traffic is
residents coming home; the `transit_am_pm_share` of 1.413 is the formal version of that. The menu is
already built for it — $18–32 platters and a $27 oxtail sandwich are family-meal price points, not
lunch-counter ones. Concretely: a family platter / feeds-two-to-three bundle priced between the
$18 curry chicken platter and the $32 oxtail platter, promoted for 17:00–21:00 pickup, sells into
the one daypart where this address beats a Bedford Ave address on *addressable* demand, because
Bedford's evening traffic is visitors who are eating out and Graham's is residents who are deciding
what to bring home. Per the sensitivity table in §6, **dinner ticket is the largest ticket-side
lever in the whole model at a 30.0% swing** — larger than lunch ticket (18.3%).

Corollary, also evidence-backed: **do not invest in weekend brunch.** Saturday+Sunday average 75% of
a weekday at this station. At Roebling it is 106%.

### Lever 2 — Delivery is structurally the right channel here, and the 800 m ring is where it pays. **Evidence-backed.**
**The number: 14,999 homes at 800 m against 4,003 at 400 m — 10,996 households reachable only by
delivery, and a reach ratio of 3.75 against Roebling's 4.51.**

Graham's catchment thins faster than Roebling's, which means the walk-in pool is comparatively more
of the total and the delivery pool comparatively less — but 10,996 incremental households is still
**2.7× the entire walk-in base.** The operator already runs a delivery-only kitchen at 356 Devoe St,
which is the correct structure: it separates delivery throughput from the counter. The lever is to
make sure the 376 Graham menu and the Devoe menu do not cannibalise each other's radius, and that
delivery pricing carries the ~28% platform commission rather than absorbing it — at an 18% delivery
share the commission drag is only **2.5% of revenue** in my model (the smallest of nine parameters),
which means delivery is close to free margin-wise *if it is priced correctly* and a real drag if it
is not.

### Lever 3 — A beer-and-wine licence to lift the dinner ticket. **Evidence-backed on the demand side, judgment on the size.**
**The numbers: `age_fit_bar` 1.118 ± 0.070 (above 1.0 by 1.7 SE); 92 active on-premises licences
within 400 m; 40.3% ± 6.3 18–34 share; and zero SLA rows at 376 Graham.**

The venue has no licence at all. The block's 18–34 share raises the D63 bar-demand ranking (1.118),
the corridor has 92 on-premises licences within 400 m, and five of the nearest competitors —
Chingoo, Good Thanks, Meet & Meat, Hummus Market, Land To Sea — hold one. A restaurant class (0240
beer/wine or 0340 full) is normal on this block and its absence is a real gap in this venue's own
offer, not the block's.

**But read the ceiling honestly.** 92 on-premises licences is **0.44× Roebling's 211**, and per
1,000 households it is 23.0 vs 53.0. The decisive split is **Additional Bar: 3 at Graham vs 36 at
Roebling** — that class marks venues with a second bar, i.e. drinking destinations. **Graham is a
corridor of restaurants that serve drinks, not a drinking corridor.** So: a beer-and-wine licence to
attach $8–14 of drinks to a $24 dinner ticket is supported by the evidence. A bar programme, late
hours, or a licence-led concept pivot is not, and D63 is explicit that `age_fit` is non-filtering and
informs ranking only.

### Lever 4 — Fold in the thin categories that are genuinely thin, and do not read "restaurant 1.86×" as headroom. **Partly evidence-backed; the negative half is the important half.**
**The numbers: childcare 0.58×, bank 0.35×, tailor_repair 0.00× (the address's `lead_category`) —
against cafe_bakery 3.33×, bar 9.24× and restaurant 1.86×.**

The honest version of this lever is mostly a *warning*, and it is the D70 guardrail:

- **`restaurant` and `cafe_bakery` are both `no_signal` in D70.** For those categories, incumbent
  counts enter the grade **in neither direction**. "Restaurant supply is only 1.86× baseline here vs
  4.42× at Roebling, so there is room" is exactly the inference D70 forbids — and `cafe_bakery`
  additionally *failed its placebo*, meaning café density tracks generic gentrification rather than
  coffee demand. **So the "keep the daytime pastry line because café is thin at Graham" idea in the
  brief has no support: café is not thin here (3.33× baseline, 16 within 400 m including Variety
  Coffee Roasters at 31 m, Dunkin at 32 m and Ando Patisserie at 44 m), and even if it were, D70 says
  not to trade on it.**
- **The genuinely thin categories are not food.** tailor_repair 0.00× (the model's actual
  `lead_category` for this address), bank 0.35×, childcare 0.58×. None is a plausible fold-in for a
  sandwich counter. Childcare at 0.58× paired with the 13.8% under-18 share is the most interesting
  *neighbourhood* fact in the table and the least actionable one for this operator.

What survives as actionable: the daytime gap at this venue is **its own**, not the block's. The
posted 11:00 open means the address's strongest transit hour — **AM peak, 2,690 entries, 36.3% of
the weekday** — passes the door with the shutter down. A breakfast empanada / Cuban coffee window
from 07:30 is a **judgment** lever (the transit number is real; whether a counter can convert
commuter flow at a station where Variety Coffee already trades 31 m away is not established), and it
competes for the same labour hours as Lever 1. I rank Lever 1 above it because the evening number is
addressable demand and the AM number is flow past a saturated café set.

### Lever 5 — Rent is the lever with the largest *certain* dollar behind it, and the pipeline is weaker than it looks. **Evidence-backed.**
**The numbers: `rent_ceiling` $8,551/mo from the shipped model; $6,709/mo at 8% of my own §6
estimate; and 159 permitted units within 400 m of which only 83 are active and 75 are stalled.**

Occupancy cost is the one line an operator controls without selling anything. The shipped model's
ceiling is **$102,614/yr = $8,551/mo** at the restaurant OCR of 0.08 — $74.63/sq ft/yr on the
whole-lot 1,375 sq ft, or $128/sq ft on an 800 sq ft demise. Against Roebling's $16,633/mo ceiling
that is 0.51×. My own bottom-up (§6) implies **$5,032/mo at 6% OCR, $6,709 at 8%, $8,386 at 10%**.
The public rent evidence is thin — no Graham-Ave-specific small-storefront comp was retrievable
(LoopNet and Crexi return 403 to automated fetch); the general Brooklyn ground-floor asking band
seen in search snippets was **$37.50–$80/sq ft/yr**, and the Graham Avenue BID's own listings on the
southern stretch show **$45/sq ft/yr**. At $45–$80/sq ft on 800 sq ft that is **$3,000–$5,333/mo**,
which sits comfortably under every ceiling above. **Judgment: rent on this block is very unlikely to
be the binding constraint, and that is the single best thing about this address relative to
Roebling** — where the same model's ceiling is 2× higher but Williamsburg asking rents are 2–3×
higher still.

On arriving residents, the number cuts against the site and should be said: 159 permitted units
within 400 m is more than Roebling's 143, but **only 83 are active and 75 are stalled (52% active),
against Roebling's 143 active and 0 stalled.** Per D72's activity test, half this pipeline is
evidence of *abandonment*, not arrival. The compensating fact is real and better: **109 units
completed within 400 m in the last 24 months against Roebling's 53** — those residents have already
moved in and are inside the 4,003.

## 7. Caveats

- **The Lion's Milk premise is unconfirmed and the evidence points the other way.** §0. If the
  operator link is real, §5's concept-specific levers need re-running.
- **`frame='street'` rows were in flight during this analysis.** `analysis.address` had **no
  `frame` column** at read time, so every row used here is a lot row by construction. When the
  street frame lands, `homes_400m`, `supply_400m` and every ratio at this address may change, and
  **no figure in this memo should be compared against a post-frame number without re-running.**
- **Supply double-count, and it is worse than at Lion's Milk.** The closed predecessor Okozushi
  still occupies two principled restaurant slots at this exact lot (`active_basis:
  inspected_384d_ago` — a closed restaurant stays "active" for 384 days), and the operator holds
  three principled clusters city-wide. 44 restaurants within 400 m is an upper bound; 35–39 is more
  plausible. Six named venues are double-counted across `restaurant` and `cafe_bakery`, three across
  `restaurant` and `bar`.
- **Census tract fusion.** Every demographic here is the **whole ACS 2023 tract** (36047049700 for
  Graham, 36047051900 for Roebling) assigned to a point. The tract is not the 400 m walk-shed, and
  the two tracts are not the same size (1,658 vs 3,283 households) — so a share difference between
  the two sites is partly a difference in what area is being averaged. The income MOEs (±$12,956
  and ±$28,506) are propagated; the share MOEs are reported but **not** propagated into the revenue
  model, which uses only the quintile.
- **The shipped model is restaurant-only and corridor-driven.** Nine of fifteen categories ship no
  revenue number at all. `revenue-v0` has **no capacity term** — no seats, square feet, hours or
  ticket — and its level is fitted to the Economic Census county mean by construction, with **no
  out-of-sample test of the level at any grain**, only of the ranking (Spearman_oos 0.7631). Its
  γ = −0.25 is agglomerative, so it scores this address down partly *for having fewer competitors*.
- **D70 forbids the headroom reading for this category.** restaurant, cafe_bakery, bar and
  convenience are all `no_signal`: incumbent counts enter in neither direction. Any "supply is thin,
  so there is room" argument about restaurants or cafés on this block is unsupported.
- **Transit figures are a summer window.** `2026-06-01..2026-08-31` weekday entrances, annualised in
  §1 by a 261/52/52 weighting. Summer is not the year, and Williamsburg summer is not Williamsburg
  February. The MTA's published 2024 annual counts (2,265,177 Graham / 8,764,921 Bedford) corroborate
  the *ratio* to within 3%.
- **Pipeline.** D72's `activity_status` separates abandoned from not-abandoned; a renewed permit is
  not a shovel. 75 stalled units within 400 m is evidence of nothing being built, not of demolition.
- **Menu prices are Uber Eats prices deflated by an assumed 1.15 platform markup.** If the platform
  markup is not 15%, every ticket in §6(b) moves with it — that is the `price_level` factor, 20.0%
  of the answer.
- **One observation cannot validate a model; it can only embarrass it.** And this one is a
  two-month-old business, which is the hardest case of all: the ramp adjustment in §6(b) is itself
  a guess.
