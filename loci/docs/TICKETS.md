## E2 · Access Engine

*Week 2* — Walk graph, multi-source Dijkstra scoring, DNCI. Ships the DNCI map — the first genuinely interesting artifact.

### Build OSMnx pedestrian walk graph
`High` · `3 pts` · `score`

network_type='walk', 4.8 km/h (80 m/min).

Extend the graph BEYOND the city boundary so cross-boundary businesses are reachable where they genuinely are — otherwise every edge hex is spuriously underserved (threat §7.7).

### Multi-source Dijkstra access engine
`Urgent` · `8 pts` · `score`

THE performance-critical design decision. Do NOT compute ~7,400 isochrones.

For each of the 15 categories, run ONE multi-source Dijkstra seeded from every POI in that category at once, cut off at threshold distance. Nodes within cutoff are 'served'.

15 traversals instead of 7,400 — minutes not hours, and it makes the 5/10/15-min sweep affordable (45 traversals total).

Emits analysis.hex_access.

### Calibrate saturating k_c per category
`Medium` · `3 pts` · `score`

s_c = 1 − exp(−n_c / k_c), calibrated so the first reachable establishment scores ≈0.55.

Essentials tighter (k≈1.25 — one grocery is nearly sufficient); food looser (one restaurant in a 10-min walk is thin, not complete). Revisit against observed count distributions. CONTEXT.md open question #2.

### DNCI: weighted geometric mean + unit tests
`Urgent` · `5 pts` · `score`

DNCI = Π (s_c + ε)^w_c, ε = 0.01.

THE METHODOLOGICAL CRUX. An arithmetic mean lets a hex with fifty restaurants and no grocery, pharmacy or laundromat score well — precisely the failure the thesis exists to detect. Only the geometric form punishes zeros.

Tests must include the adversarial case: 50 restaurants + 0 essentials must score LOW. If that test passes under an arithmetic mean, the test is wrong.

### Run 5/10/15-minute threshold sweep
`Medium` · `2 pts` · `score`

10 min (800m) is the headline. 5 min (400m) and 15 min (1200m) as sensitivity and for comparability with the 15-minute-city literature.

`High` · `3 pts` · `milestone`

Week-2 exit criterion and the first genuinely interesting artifact. Sequential palette. Sanity-check against local knowledge before proceeding to E3.

## E3 · Residual and Panel

*Week 3* — Supply model, residual extraction, LODES panel, growth test with its three mandatory checks. Ships the empirical result and the top-20 list.

### Fit the supply model
`Urgent` · `5 pts` · `model`

DNCI ~ pop_density + median_hh_income + transit_access + commercial_zoning_capacity + land_fraction + borough FE.

Check prediction P1: the residual must have meaningful spread. If R² > 0.9 there is nothing left to explain and the thesis has no room to be true — report that honestly rather than tuning until it isn't.

### Moran's I + spatial error/lag model
`High` · `5 pts` · `model,rigor`

MANDATORY, not optional. Compute Moran's I on residuals; if significant (it will be), re-estimate with pysal spreg and report both.

OLS standard errors on gridded urban data are wrong. Shipping a p-value without this is shipping a p-value you cannot defend.

### Residual extraction + opportunity score
`High` · `3 pts` · `model`

opportunity = (−u_h)⁺ × transit_access × (ResidFAR − BuiltFAR)⁺.

Underserved AND transit-connected AND room to build. Any one of the three missing disqualifies the hex.

### LODES WAC annual panel loader 2002–2023
`High` · `5 pts` · `panel,ingest`

NY state WAC files, target NAICS 4451/4461/7224/7225/8121/8123/4441/6244. Block-level, 22 vintages.

Blocked on the LODES8 block-vintage verification (E1).

### LODES block → hex apportionment
`High` · `3 pts` · `panel,grid`

Areal apportionment of block-level jobs onto H3 cells.

Document the proxy assumption loudly: LODES counts JOBS, not establishments. A ten-employee supermarket and a one-employee laundromat are not comparable units (threat §7.4).

### Quantify pre-2020 LODES allocation bias
`High` · `5 pts` · `panel,rigor`

Discovered while resolving the LODES vintage check.

LODES8 puts every year on 2020 blocks, but historical years got there by ALLOCATION, not observation: when a 2010 block splits, each job is assigned to a child block at random with probability proportional to AREA share. Census: 'the allocation is a statistical process and may not result in a distribution of jobs that exactly matches the areal distribution.'

Area-proportional is a poor assumption in NYC — a block splitting into a park half and a commercial half spreads jobs by area, putting employment in the park.

This is BIAS, not noise: the error concentrates where blocks were split, blocks split where development happened, and development is the outcome variable.

Size the exposure, test whether H3 res-9 aggregation actually absorbs it, and if not restrict the strongest claims to 2020+ observed data. CONTEXT.md threat 7.4(b).

### Validate LODES 2023 against establishment counts
`High` · `3 pts` · `panel,validation`

Correlate the 2023 LODES cross-section against 2023 DOHMH/DCWP establishment counts per hex. Report the correlation.

This is what licenses the jobs→establishments proxy. Without it the whole panel rests on an unexamined assumption.

### Assemble outcome variables
`High` · `5 pts` · `ingest`

Δlog population, Δlog households (ACS); Δlog ZORI (Zillow, ZIP→hex); permitted residential units (DOB/HPD).

ZIP→hex crosswalk by population weighting — CONTEXT.md open question #4. Label rent as the weakest of the four outcomes in the memo and mean it.

### Main growth regression (prediction P2)
`Urgent` · `5 pts` · `model`

Δy_{h,t0→t1} = β·u_{h,t0} + γ'X_{h,t0} + α_borough + ε, t0=2013 t1=2023.

P2 predicts β < 0 (more negative residual → more subsequent growth). A null or wrong-signed result is a real answer: it would mean gaps persist because they reflect durable demand suppression, not latent opportunity. Report it either way.

### Pre-trend test (2003→2013)
`Urgent` · `3 pts` · `model,rigor`

Estimate the same spec on the PRIOR decade. If u_2013 also 'predicts' 2003→2013 growth, parallel trends is broken and the causal reading is unavailable.

Report it whichever way it comes out. This is the check a serious reader will ask for first.

### Placebo outcome
`High` · `2 pts` · `model,rigor`

An outcome with no plausible mechanism — e.g. change in share of population aged 65+ — must return a null. If it doesn't, the specification is picking up generic neighborhood trajectory rather than the retail channel.

### MAUP sweep at res 8 and res 10
`High` · `3 pts` · `model,rigor`

Re-run the whole chain at two other resolutions; report coefficient stability. If conclusions flip with grid size, say so plainly (threat §7.3).

### Tier-weight sensitivity analysis
`Medium` · `3 pts` · `model,score`

Tier weights (0.40/0.20/0.25/0.15) are judgment, not derived. Sweep plausible alternatives and report whether the top-20 list is stable under reweighting. CONTEXT.md open question #1.

### Top-20 list + zoning-artifact audit
`Urgent` · `3 pts` · `model,infra`

Produce the ranked list of transit-rich underserved hexes.

HARD GATE: manually inspect for park edges, industrial zones and cemetery blocks. Any present means the commercial-zoning control failed — fix the model, do not hand-remove the rows.

Also check it contains ≥3 genuine surprises. A list of only obvious places means the residual isn't doing any work.

### Residual convergence test
`Low` · `3 pts` · `panel,model`

QUESTIONS.md T5 — does the gap close on its own?

Over 2002–2023, do hexes with a negative residual at t converge toward their peers by t+k (retail employment growth regressed on the lagged residual, borough FE)?

Why it matters: if the market already corrects undersupply, the opportunity is TIMING, not location — a different product and a different memo. Cheap once the LODES panel exists; pull forward if W3 has slack, otherwise it slides to Phase 5.

### Retail lead/lag timing test
`Low` · `3 pts` · `panel,model`

QUESTIONS.md T6 — does retail lead or lag rooftops?

Cross-correlate annual per-hex changes in LODES retail employment with changes in population at leads and lags of 1–5 years.

Why it matters: 'retail follows rooftops' is the assumption the whole residual design rests on (D1). This is the one place it gets tested rather than asserted.

CAVEATS to state: ACS 5-year smoothing limits timing resolution to roughly half-decades; LODES counts jobs, not storefronts (M2).

### SHIP W3: empirical result + top-20
`High` · `2 pts` · `milestone`

Week-3 exit criterion.

## E4 · Validation and Artifact

*Week 4* — Coverage-bias validation (prediction P3), maps, charts, evidence cards, memo, reproducibility. Ships the public artifact.

### Design stratified coverage validation sample
`Urgent` · `3 pts` · `validation,critical`

Prediction P3 and threat §7.1 — the one that can invalidate everything.

OSM/Overture undercount small business in lower-income and immigrant neighborhoods: exactly the areas the thesis flags as underserved. If the undercount is strong there and weak in Park Slope, the 'retail gap' is a data gap wearing a costume.

Draw hexes at random within each income decile, and cross-stratify by foreign-born share (ACS) so borough / immigrant-neighborhood contrasts in the residual (QUESTIONS.md X3) can be separated from coverage bias. Fix n per stratum against the 5,000-call budget before spending anything.

### Run Google Places ground-truth enumeration
`Urgent` · `5 pts` · `validation,critical,blocker`

Enumerate ground truth for the sampled hexes. Fields: id/name/location/types ONLY — anything more reprices the SKU.

Enforce the call budget in code. Populate analysis.coverage_validation.

### DOHMH-anchored undercount calibration
`Urgent` · `5 pts` · `validation,critical`

Within the food tier the true count is effectively known (DOHMH is a near-census). Measure OSM/Overture discrepancy vs DOHMH per hex, then use that curve to estimate expected undercount in the other tiers where no census exists.

This is the strongest available mitigation and it costs nothing extra.
